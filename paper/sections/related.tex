\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Music source separation and distillation.}
Four-stem separation (\op{vocals}, \op{drums}, \op{bass}, \op{other}) on uncompressed benchmarks such as MUSDB18-HQ~\citep{MUSDB18HQ} and the Sound Demixing Challenge (MDX23)~\citep{fabbro2024sdx} is led by hybrid time--frequency transformers (HTDemucs)~\citep{rouard2023hybrid} and band-split models such as Band-Split RNN (BSRNN)~\citep{luo2023music} and BS-RoFormer~\citep{lu2024bsroformer}. These studio models achieve high signal-to-distortion ratios (SDR)~\citep{vincent2006performance}, but their memory use and multi-second GPU runtimes rule out per-stream CPU radio deployment. Shrinking a $2048$-point STFT separator naively loses vocal harmonic resolution ($21.5$\,Hz/bin blurs low fundamentals and vibrato) and causes phase cancellation between stems when unconstrained masks drift apart. \sone{} uses knowledge distillation~\citep{hinton2015distilling} to narrow the gap: we run offline teachers (\op{HTDemucs-ft} and \op{BS-RoFormer-1297}) on Creative Commons audio from MTG-Jamendo~\citep{bogdanov2019mtg} and Song Describer~\citep{manco2023song}, and train a compact $4096$-FFT ($10.77$\,Hz/bin), $64$-band Dual-Axis Transformer with bounded complex masks, energy-weighted Wiener mixture projection, multi-resolution STFT plus $128$-mel loss~\citep{yamamoto2020parallel}, and vocal cosine phase regularisation. To grow \op{Cochlea-Pro} into \op{Cochlea-Ultra} without training from scratch, we apply function-preserving Net2Net width and depth expansion~\citep{chen2016net2net}.

\paragraph{Metrical, vocal, and harmonic analysis.}
Joint beat and downbeat tracking with temporal convolutional networks (TCNs) and bidirectional recurrent layers was established by \citet{bock2020deconstruct}, and \citet{foscarin2024beat} showed with \emph{Beat This!} that shift-tolerant supervision can replace Dynamic Bayesian Network (DBN) post-processing. DJ transition planning also needs frame-level active singing, singer breath pauses ($\ge 0.28$\,s gaps suitable for echo throws), eight-bar phrase boundaries, $24$-class major/minor key, and concert-pitch tuning offsets in cents. Public datasets with all seven targets are small, so a baseline TCN--BiGRU quickly memorises track keys and tempos. \op{Tympanum} counters data scarcity with vinyl-speed equivariant resampling, which transforms pitch class, sub-semitone tuning, and all five temporal label streams analytically with no label drift. At inference, neural $50$\,Hz activations are combined with a deterministic multi-octave comb grid over spectral flux.

\paragraph{Automatic DJ transitions.}
Rule-based DJ engines range from beat-matched crossfaders~\citep{cliff2000hang} and transition-point selectors~\citep{bittner2017automatic} to genre-specific drum-and-bass mixers~\citep{vandeveire2018raw}. Deterministic templates keep audio clean and preserve bar lock, but fixed fader curves cannot adapt to unusual vocal intros or flag harmonic clashes. Differentiable mixers trained with adversarial waveform critics~\citep{chen2022automatic} learn fader and EQ curves from data, yet adversarial losses do not easily enforce discrete rules such as keeping a vocalist uncut mid-syllable or holding accompaniment level until incoming audio arrives. Drawing on the conflict-monitoring role of the anterior cingulate cortex~\citep{botvinick2001conflict}, \sone{} pairs a beat-synchronous planner (\op{Cortex}) with a $30$-defect counterfactual monitor (\op{Cingulate}) for Best-of-$K$ plan selection, wrapped around deterministic vocal-onset-locked DSP rendering and untouched master splicing.
