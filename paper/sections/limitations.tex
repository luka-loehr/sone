\section{Limitations and Broader Impact}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Observed limitations.}
Inspecting plans, separated stems, and rendered mixes across all four \sone{} tiers leaves six concrete limitations open:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Playlist-constrained hard cuts:} If an outgoing track sings continuously right up to its final downbeat with no instrumental outro or breath gap, \op{Cortex} must pick an on-beat cut or a short sneak rather than layering two voices. \op{Cingulate} flags \op{CutNotTransition}, but turning those pairs into longer blends requires upstream playlist reordering or bar-loop extension before the vocal ends.
  \item \emph{CPU footprint vs.\ $>100$\,M offline GPU separators:} Two-stage \op{BS-RoFormer-1297}~\citep{lu2024bsroformer} ($12.98$\,dB) + \op{HTDemucs-ft}~\citep{rouard2023hybrid} distillation and Net2Net scaling to $10$ Dual-Axis blocks ($29.18$\,M parameters) narrow the gap, yet \op{Cochlea-Pro v2} ($7.98$\,dB) and \op{Cochlea-Ultra} ($7.95$\,dB MUSDB, $6.93$\,dB broadcast) still trail offline GPU ensembles on dense stereo reverb tails and heavily processed vocals. Our gated $260$--$6200$\,Hz zero-phase mid-formant bridge compensates for telephone-filtered vocal lines routed into \op{other}, but it is a targeted DSP fallback rather than true source separation.
  \item \emph{Key-head overfitting in \op{Tympanum-Pro}:} Scaling \op{Tympanum} from $305$\,K to $1.47$\,M parameters improved downbeat F1, voice accuracy, and tuning MAE, while raw $24$-class key accuracy fell from $43.0\%$ to $24.6\%$ on the small key-labelled validation split. Larger key-annotated training sets or stronger head regularisation are needed before dropping chroma-based track key checks.
  \item \emph{Global SDR degeneracy on silent boundary stems:} On held-out track \op{7yoqSl} (ground-truth vocal RMS $-107.8$\,dBFS), standard full-window BSSEval SDR~\citep{vincent2006performance} drops to $-15.2$\,dB on \op{Pro v2} and $-29.3$\,dB on \op{Ultra} even though residual vocal energy sits at $-93.0$\,dBFS and $-78.7$\,dBFS respectively. Evaluating boundary separators requires MDX23 active-chunk median SDR~\citep{fabbro2024sdx} alongside silent-stem RMS floors in dBFS.
  \item \emph{Grammar ceiling, tempo range, and non-$4/4$ meter:} \op{Cortex} plans inside a deterministic four-beat ruler ($4/4$ meter, rational beat ratios within $\pm 6.8\%$ stretch). It cannot generate turntable scratch routines, rhythmic stutter edits, or transitions in $3/4$ or $6/8$ meter.
  \item \emph{Single-listener preference set:} Both blind listening rounds and the $152$ \op{owner.jsonl} labels come from one experienced listener rather than a multi-listener MUSHRA or MOS panel. Because \op{owner.jsonl} is folded into \op{v3/v4} alignment, out-of-sample neural generalisation is checked on unseen \op{val.jsonl.gz} ($n=5{,}932$) rather than an independent human rater panel.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Broader impact and data licensing.}
Unlike generative music models that synthesise new waveforms from copyrighted catalogues, \sone{} acts purely as a local mixing controller. Inside a licensed radio pipeline or personal player, it adjusts stem gains, EQ, and timing only across the $\sim 24$\,s boundary and streams original artist recordings bit-for-bit across the rest of every song. No audio samples are stored in model weights or repository files. The distillation corpora MTG-Jamendo~\citep{bogdanov2019mtg} and Song Describer~\citep{manco2023song} carry Creative Commons licences, whereas MUSDB18-HQ~\citep{MUSDB18HQ} carries non-commercial research terms (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Accordingly, checkpoints trained on MUSDB18-HQ are released under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for non-commercial research use, while the Apache-2.0 Rust and WebAssembly runtime contains no copyrighted audio.
