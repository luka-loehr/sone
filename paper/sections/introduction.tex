\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A continuous radio stream or automated DJ player joins an outgoing recording (Deck~A) to an incoming recording (Deck~B) over four to sixteen bars. Doing this cleanly is harder than crossfading two files. Beats and downbeats must line up within a few milliseconds even when a live drummer drifts; tempo must match without shifting pitch; the entry structure has to fit both arrangements (layering Deck~B's opening vocal over Deck~A's instrumental outro, or bringing percussion in quietly under a verse); conflicting basslines and mid-range chords must be carved out; stem bleed before a singer enters has to stay silent; and outgoing vocals should end on a natural breath with a tempo-synced echo tail. Most of every song lies outside the transition window $[u_0, u_1]$. For that remaining $85$--$90\%$ of the track, listeners expect the original stereo master untouched.

End-to-end generative audio models fit this problem poorly for three reasons. First, passing full recordings through neural waveform or latent codecs colours non-transition audio and leaves watery, phase-smeared vocals on dense mixes. Second, generative audio backbones need hundreds of millions of parameters and dedicated GPUs, whereas streaming services with thousands of concurrent personalised stations need transitions rendered asynchronously on a fraction of a cloud CPU core or in client-side WebAssembly. Third, DJ transitions obey sharp metrical and perceptual constraints: bar phase, sixteenth-note vocal onset locks, relative minor/major harmonic equivalence, and full-stem continuity after the drop. Small regression smoothing errors on any of these constraints sound immediately wrong even when average $L_1$ loss looks low.

We separate perception and planning from audio rendering. Four compact neural organs run only inside the $\sim 24$\,s boundary window, while deterministic signal processing handles time-stretching, alignment, mixing, and splicing. Outside $[u_0, u_1]$, \sone{} streams the gain-matched original stereo PCM bit-for-bit across raised-cosine seams and glides Deck~B back to its native tempo. This keeps neural colouration off the body of every song and cuts separation compute by roughly $7.5\times$ on a $3$\,min track.

All four neural organs are packed into a single \op{model.safetensors} file and named after the human auditory-to-executive pathway: \emph{Cochlea} splits stereo mixtures into four phase-coherent stems, \emph{Tympanum} extracts $50$\,Hz metrical and vocal-breath structure, \emph{Cortex} plans beat-synchronous stem trajectories, and \emph{Cingulate} monitors $30$ defect modes~\citep{botvinick2001conflict} to rerank candidate plans. We call the family \sone{} after Stevens' psychoacoustic unit of loudness~\citep{stevens1936scale}, because our listening audits repeatedly showed that clean transitions depend on loudness continuity, vocal phase coherence, and tight beat synchronisation.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Four-tier unified neuro-symbolic DJ family (\S\ref{sec:method}):} Sone-6.5M ($6.52$\,M parameters, $13.12$\,MB FP16), Sone-v2-Base ($13.61$\,M, $27.36$\,MB), Sone-Pro ($26.37$\,M, $52.90$\,MB), and Sone-Ultra ($35.48$\,M, $71.12$\,MB). All four share one zero-copy Rust/WebAssembly wire format (\op{sone-infer/1}) and run at $4.8\%$--$36.4\%$ of one Intel Xeon vCPU core per $3$\,min song.
  \item \textbf{Phase-coherent Dual-Axis \op{Cochlea} and two-stage teacher cascade (\S\ref{sec:stems}):} $4096$-FFT, $64$-band separation scaled across \op{Base} ($6$ blocks, $d=80$), \op{Pro v2} ($8$ blocks, $d=128$, $7.98$\,dB MUSDB vocal SDR), and function-preserving Net2Net \op{Ultra} ($10$ blocks, $d=160$, $29.18$\,M parameters, $6.93$\,dB held-out broadcast mean SDR). Bounded complex masks are projected onto exact mixture consistency ($\|\sum_m \hat{s}_m - x\|_\infty < -148$\,dB) and distilled from a two-stage GPU teacher: \op{BS-RoFormer-1297}~\citep{lu2024bsroformer} ($12.98$\,dB vocal SDR) followed by \op{HTDemucs-ft}~\citep{rouard2023hybrid} on the vocal-free instrumental residual.
  \item \textbf{Vinyl-speed equivariant \op{Tympanum} and Newton drum-slope lock (\S\ref{sec:ear}, \S\ref{sec:ruler}):} Exact pitch, key, tuning and tempo equivariant resampling ($\alpha = 2^{\Delta s/12}$) trained on $565$ tracks ($0.865$ raw beat F1), paired with an onset-flux comb grid ($12/12$ exact BPM locks) and closed-form joint Newton drum-slope plus phase-nudge alignment that holds rendered beat drift within $0.73$\,ms ($0.29$\,ms mean across $10$ mixes, $0.00$\,ms lag).
  \item \textbf{\op{Cortex v4}, 12-mode counterfactual \op{Cingulate v4}, and blind-audit fixes (\S\ref{sec:beat}, \S\ref{sec:res_beat}):} Two rounds of blind listening uncovered six failure modes: live-drummer tempo drift, missed vocal overlaps over instrumental outros, post-drop vocal and melody holes, relative Camelot false clashes, pre-vocal stem-bleed fuzz, and premature triggers or dropped telephone-filtered vocal lines on dense half-time handoffs (resolved via sustained-singing onset filtering and gated $260$--$6200$\,Hz zero-phase mid-formant vocal continuity bridging). Held-out timing MAE falls to $0.127$\,bars ($100.0\%$ owner recipe match on $106$ accepted mixes).
\end{itemize}
